No purchase, no account, no launcher

Enemy Territory was released as a free standalone game in 2003 and it stayed free. You do not buy it, you do not make an account, and you do not install a storefront to run it. You download a client, type in a name, and connect.

It is twenty-odd years old, which is the best thing about it: it installs in minutes, it runs on the laptop you already own, and a match takes about the length of a lunch break.

How a match worksTwo sides, one list of jobs

One team has a to-do list. The other team says no.

Every map is a sequence of objectives with a clock on it. Attackers work down the list — blow this wall, steal these documents, escort this tank. Defenders only have to run out the clock. When the objective moves, the whole fight moves with it, so there is always an obvious place to be.

Allies

Usually attacking. Loud, forward, and on a deadline.

Axis

Usually defending. Hold the line until the timer runs out.

You pick a class when you spawn and can change it whenever you like. Shooting well is one way to be useful, and comfortably the least important one:

Medic
Revive teammates and hand out health. The easiest class to be genuinely valuable in on day one.
Engineer
Plants and defuses the explosives, builds the bridges and guns. Most objectives literally cannot happen without one.
Field Ops
Resupplies ammo and calls in artillery and air strikes on whatever is holding your team up.
Covert Ops
Wears the enemy's uniform, spots mines, and opens shortcuts nobody is watching.
Soldier
Carries the heavy weapons — the machine gun, the flamethrower, the rocket launcher.

Everything you do earns experience: bandaging people, building things, hitting shots. Play enough and your class gets better at its own job.

Questions you are about to ask

I have never played a shooter.

Then play medic and follow someone. Reviving a teammate scores you more than a kill does, and half the server is running on twenty years of muscle memory anyway — nobody is keeping score of your aim.

Will it run on my laptop?

It is a 2003 game. If your machine can open a browser with thirty tabs, it can run this.

Is anyone actually on?

Bots keep both teams populated at all hours, so you can drop in alone and still get a real match. When we are on, we are on together — that part gets organised in the Discord.

Is this legal / safe?

The game has been free to download since release, and ET: Legacy is open source. There is no anti-cheat driver and nothing that wants your card details.
